Kåre Vernby

Professor of Political Science
Stockholm University

I am a professor of political science at Stockholm University, where I teach comparative politics and research methods.

I study what ties people and places to democratic politics, and why those ties are stronger for some than for others. This has led to work on immigrants’ voting rights and representation, voter turnout, the radical right, and the local origins of national party systems. I answer these questions mostly with register data, natural experiments, and experiments in the field and in surveys. While much of my work uses Sweden as a case, I also study other advanced industrial democracies, such as the United States and France. My work has appeared in journals such as the American Political Science Review, the American Journal of Political Science, and the Journal of Politics.

Current research

Opportunity on the Move

Swedish Research Council 2025–2029, with Rafaela Dancygier, Sirus Dehdari, David Laitin and Moritz Marbach

How does growing up in, and moving to or from, a place shape political behavior and attitudes in adulthood? The project combines the residential histories in Swedish register data with a survey designed to capture what the registers do not. Following our 2025 article on emigration and the radical right, it also continues to investigate how internal migration contributes to the growing geographical divide in politics.

Political ambition in a hostile climate

With Nazita Lajevardi and Moa Mårtensson

Does a political climate marked by polarization, threats and harassment change who is willing to seek office? We use survey experiments on citizens and politicians in Sweden and the United States, conventional surveys, and interviews in both countries, to find out how hostility affects the willingness to run and whose ambition it suppresses.

The Local Roots of the Nationalization of Politics

Swedish Research Council, with Jan Teorell

How did fragmented, locally dominated polities become the national party systems of today? The literature has studied the spread of the same parties across districts in national elections. We argue that this depended on the parties’ spread across levels of government: national party systems were built by local political entrepreneurs who chose to run under a national party brand, and by national governments that rewarded the localities where they did. We test the argument with local and national election results from twelve European countries, a natural experiment in French local elections (with Emanuel Coman), and archival data on grants and elections in interwar Sweden (with Magnus Anker).
